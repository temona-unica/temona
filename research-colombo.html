Overview

Two research lines are currently active: non-stationary transport, which extends irreversible thermodynamics and molecular dynamics beyond the steady state, and coupled heat, mass, and charge transport in multicomponent and ionic fluids.

Non-Stationary Transport

General Framework

Transport phenomena provide a unified description of the spatio-temporal evolution of conserved quantities such as heat, mass, and electric charge. Classical formulations, from the Fourier, Fick, and Ohm laws to Onsager’s theory of irreversible thermodynamics and the Green–Kubo relations, rest on the assumptions of stationary states and infinitesimal deviations from equilibrium.

These assumptions become inadequate in systems where temperature, concentration, or electric potential gradients are intrinsically time-dependent and evolve on timescales comparable to, or shorter than, microscopic relaxation times. In such regimes, memory effects, delayed responses, nonlinear behavior, and the breakdown of local equilibrium emerge. The study of non-stationary transport is therefore relevant both to the foundations of nonequilibrium thermodynamics and to applications in nanoelectronics, thermoelectrics, microfluidics, and advanced materials.

Theoretical Framework

The project adopts irreversible thermodynamics in both its classical and extended formulations. Classical Irreversible Thermodynamics (CIT) is based on the local equilibrium hypothesis and on linear force–flux relations. It describes coupled transport and entropy production near equilibrium: transport coefficients are instantaneous, fluxes respond locally to their conjugate forces, and the evolution equations are parabolic.

Since the time-dependent regimes addressed here lie at or beyond the limits of validity of CIT, Extended Irreversible Thermodynamics (EIT) plays a central role. EIT treats fluxes as independent state variables and extends the entropy functional accordingly. In this way it incorporates finite relaxation times, temporal nonlocality, and hyperbolic transport, and provides a consistent thermodynamic description of memory effects, finite propagation speeds, and frequency-dependent transport coefficients. The two frameworks will be systematically compared in order to identify their domains of validity and to clarify the thermodynamic meaning of non-stationary transport coefficients, entropy production, and dissipation under time-dependent driving.

Computational Methodology

The microscopic foundations of non-stationary transport will be investigated through large-scale nonequilibrium molecular dynamics simulations, which resolve microscopic timescales, correlation functions, and relaxation processes. Time-dependent thermal, chemical, or electrostatic gradients will be imposed to probe the system response beyond the steady state. From the atomic trajectories, time-resolved fluxes, correlation functions, and spectral transport coefficients will be extracted, giving access to memory kernels and frequency-dependent responses.

This approach links macroscopic constitutive laws to microscopic dynamics. It also assesses possible inertial effects and discriminates between ballistic, diffusive, and propagative regimes. Combined with CIT and EIT, it aims at a coherent multiscale description of non-stationary transport, grounded in first principles and applicable to complex material systems.

Research Goals

The project aims at a rigorous characterization of transport under time-dependent forcing, with gradients evolving on timescales comparable to microscopic relaxation. It also seeks quantitative criteria establishing when the steady-state and near-equilibrium paradigms remain valid and when explicitly dynamical constitutive descriptions are required. Specifically:

  • To map, in a systematically parameterized manner, the breakdown of Fourier-, Fick-, and Ohm-type laws for heat, mass, and charge transport. The breakdown will be quantified in terms of characteristic times, lengths, and forcing spectra, and the dimensionless control parameters governing the onset of memory, delay, and nonlinearity will be identified.
  • To formulate and validate generalized transport relations consistent with irreversible thermodynamics beyond the instantaneous-response limit. These relations will be expressed through memory kernels and frequency-dependent transport coefficients, allowing a unified treatment of different conserved quantities and material classes.
  • To connect these macroscopic descriptors to their microscopic origins through nonequilibrium molecular dynamics under prescribed time-dependent gradients. This involves identifying relaxation mechanisms, reconstructing effective memory kernels, and discriminating between diffusive (parabolic) and propagative (hyperbolic) regimes. Particular attention will be given to finite signal propagation, inertial effects, and the parameters controlling the transition between regimes.
  • To extend the thermodynamic analysis of dissipation to time-dependent driving, focusing on entropy balance and entropy production in the spectral domain. Under periodic and stochastic forcing, the project will clarify how phase lags, resonance-like features, and apparent reversibility affect dissipation, stability, and the efficiency of coupled transport processes.
  • The final objective is a consistent framework for non-stationary transport. This framework will classify response regimes (stationary, quasi-stationary, strongly dynamical), provide operational validity criteria for constitutive laws, and translate these criteria into guidance for applications in advanced electronics, energy conversion, and complex materials.

Coupled Transport Phenomena

General Framework

In multicomponent fluids, and in ionic fluids in particular, heat conduction, mass diffusion, and electrical transport are not independent processes. Temperature gradients can induce mass and charge fluxes, and concentration or electrostatic potential gradients can generate heat currents. These are the Soret, Dufour, Seebeck, and Peltier effects, and they coexist with the direct Fourier, Fick, and Ohm regimes. The cross-effects are small and difficult to isolate experimentally. Their computational estimate through Green–Kubo or nonequilibrium molecular dynamics approaches also requires long simulation times. A consistent and efficient description of coupled transport is relevant to electrochemistry, ionic thermoelectrics, microfluidics, heat-transfer fluids, and energy materials.

Theoretical Framework: Linear Non-Equilibrium Thermodynamics

The starting point is the Onsager formulation of non-equilibrium thermodynamics, together with the microscopic definitions of heat, mass, and charge currents. Combining the phenomenological relations with the conservation laws yields evolution equations for the temperature and density profiles. This goes beyond the standard expression of steady-state fluxes. A central concept is the generalized diffusivity. Its introduction allows the coupled equations to be formally decoupled, so that the relaxation of initial step-like profiles can be expressed analytically as a sum of exponential terms. The approach has been developed for each pair of coupled mechanisms:

  • Heat–mass coupling: the generalized thermal diffusivity includes the effect of mass flow on thermal transport. It is therefore the quantity accessible to experiment whenever coupling is present.
  • Heat–charge coupling: the generalized thermal diffusivity adds to the ordinary thermal diffusivity a term set by the electrical conductivities and the Peltier coefficient.
  • Mass–charge coupling: the dynamics is governed by a generalized diffusivity and an effective frictional term of electrostatic origin, which vanishes for uncharged species.

Computational Methodology

The theory is validated through approach-to-equilibrium molecular dynamics (AEMD), a method developed for thermal conductivity in solids and here generalized to coupled transport in fluids. The system relaxes freely from an imposed step-like profile, and fitting its transient evolution to the analytical solutions yields the generalized coefficients without requiring a steady state. Benchmark systems include binary Lennard–Jones liquid mixtures and aqueous NaCl solutions.

Main Results

  • Lennard–Jones mixtures, heat–mass coupling: AEMD reproduces Soret coefficients obtained by nonequilibrium techniques and provides the first quantitative estimate of the generalized thermal diffusivity. No rectification effect was observed. Advanced Theory and Simulations 7(4), 2300849 (2024)
  • NaCl solutions, heat–charge coupling: concurrent and discordant thermal/electrostatic gradients are non-equivalent and give rise to a rectification behavior. The behavior is traced to Soret-like thermal transport and to the alteration of the water microstructure. Applied Physics Letters 124(12), 122203 (2024)
  • Charged binary mixtures, mass–charge coupling: the electrostatic frictional term is non-zero, and both generalized coefficients depend significantly on density and temperature. Advanced Theory and Simulations 8(6), 2401371 (2025)

Perspectives

The main ongoing effort is to develop a unified dynamical description in which heat, mass, and charge transport are treated simultaneously in multicomponent ionic mixtures. In this description:

  • Fourier, Fick, and Ohm laws emerge as limiting cases of a single thermodynamic structure, and all cross-effects are encoded in a single dynamical matrix.
  • The electrostatic contribution is expected to separate a fast Debye-type relaxation mode from a set of slower diffusive electroneutral modes. This separation would provide a formal basis for the quasi-electroneutral approximation and identify its limits.

Further work will apply this framework, through AEMD, to realistic electrolytes and ionic liquids. It will also assess the validity of the linear, mean-field description at small length scales, where it connects with the extended formulations adopted for non-stationary transport. Finally, it will examine how compositional asymmetries can be used to tune transport responses in materials for energy conversion and thermal management.
