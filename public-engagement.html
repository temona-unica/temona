Our Commitment

At TEMONA, our research explores the microscopic origins that shape the properties of condensed matter systems. Yet we firmly believe that scientific discovery does not belong to specialists alone. It concerns every member of society, because our collective choices, personal lives, and self-understanding are deeply bound up with scientific knowledge and its technological applications. Our dedication to Public Engagement (PE) springs directly from this vision.

More precisely, we view PE as an essential pillar of our scientific mission, guided by clear core principles:

  • Science as a Common Good: we hold that access to scientific knowledge, at least in the form of deep understanding, is a fundamental right of every individual, regardless of age, background, or profession.
  • Openness and Rigor: we view science as a rational exploration of the natural world, whose true credibility rests on transparency, sharing, and a clear awareness of its own boundaries.

Our initiatives connect with three distinct audiences, each playing a vital role in the social circulation of knowledge:

  1. Secondary-school teachers, reached through professional development courses and expository articles, who act as key mediators bringing physics to life for new generations.
  2. University students, with whom we explore the intersections between scientific knowledge and other forms of rational inquiry on shared argumentative ground.
  3. Adult citizens in lifelong learning programs, for whom grasping how science is built is a fundamental pillar of informed citizenship at any stage of life.

Across all these channels, our goal goes far beyond merely transmitting facts to be accepted on authority. We aim to cultivate a critical capacity: empowering people to recognize how scientific knowledge is constructed, what it can legitimately claim, and where its competence naturally ends.

University course

La struttura del reale (The Structure of Reality): 48-hour course on four topics (epistemology of the scientific method; modern cosmology; artificial intelligence and the natural sciences; science and technology), combining lectures with guided-discussion laboratories.

Il metodo scientifico (The Scientific Method): how science formulates, tests and revises its claims about Nature.

Professional development of secondary-school teachers

Istituto Lombardo, Accademia di Scienze e Lettere (Milan) March–May 2027

Argomenti di fisica moderna (Topics in Modern Physics): 18-hour course on the physics of quanta, the statistical foundations of thermodynamics and special relativity, each reconstructed as a path from crisis to new theory.

Series of expository articles on the essential quantum physics, thermodynamics and quantum statistics. Latest article: Schrödinger 1926: l’equazione che la fisica cercava, Giornale di Fisica 67(3), 169–201 (2026) (10.1393/gdf/i2026-10652-4).
