Overview

This research line uses atomistic simulations, from density functional theory to machine-learned interatomic potentials, to understand how vibrational energy is stored, transported, and dissipated in materials. Four themes are currently active: phonon dynamics out of equilibrium, thermal and thermoelectric transport, coating materials for the Einstein Telescope, and accelerated simulation methods that support the other three.

Phonon dynamics out of equilibrium

When a material is driven out of equilibrium, for instance by an ultrafast laser pulse, the excess energy is first deposited in a few vibrational modes and then spreads to the others through anharmonic coupling. We study the channels and timescales of this redistribution and how they depend on structure, dimensionality, and temperature. Molecular dynamics with mode-resolved temperatures and energy fluxes, combined with anharmonic lattice dynamics, links the microscopic decay processes to the signals measured in time-resolved spectroscopy. The systems studied range from bulk and nanostructured semiconductors to liquid water and ice, where we follow the relaxation of excited intramolecular vibrations through librational and hydrogen-bond modes.

Methods: non-equilibrium molecular dynamics, normal-mode projection, anharmonic lattice dynamics, phonon lifetimes.

Thermal and thermoelectric transport

We study heat and charge transport in materials with complex structure, including conductive metal-organic frameworks, layered transition-metal dichalcogenides, π-conjugated molecular crystals, and lead-free double perovskites. Lattice thermal conductivity is computed from first-principles force constants through the Boltzmann transport equation and from molecular dynamics, with attention to higher-order anharmonicity and temperature effects. Combined with electronic transport calculations, these results guide strategies such as doping, charge modulation, and nanostructuring to improve thermoelectric performance. The work on two-dimensional MOFs is carried out together with Prof. Claudio Melis.

Methods: density functional theory, phonon Boltzmann transport equation, equilibrium and non-equilibrium molecular dynamics, electronic transport.

Coating materials for the Einstein Telescope

The Einstein Telescope, a planned third-generation gravitational-wave observatory, will need mirror coatings with very low thermal noise, which depends on the mechanical and vibrational properties of the oxide layers. We study candidate coating oxides (GeO2, Ta2O5, TiO2, SiO2, HfO2) in several crystalline phases, computing their structural and vibrational properties from first principles. In parallel, we assess how accurately machine-learned foundation potentials reproduce these properties, with the aim of reaching the system sizes needed to model the amorphous layers used in real mirrors.

Methods: density functional theory, lattice dynamics, machine-learned foundation potentials.

Accelerated and data-driven atomistic modeling

Machine-learned interatomic potentials and semi-empirical methods extend the length and time scales of atomistic simulations, provided their accuracy is checked against first-principles references. We develop and validate ChIMES potentials for silicon and for point defects in Cs2NaYbCl6, where they speed up a defect workflow refined with DFT, and we work on Chebyshev polynomials for accelerated MD simulations. We also benchmark universal potentials for phonons and thermal transport in layered dichalcogenides. DFTB simulations extend the accessible system sizes further, and DFT calculations support experimental studies of photocatalytic heterostructures.

Methods: ChIMES and universal machine-learned potentials, Chebyshev polynomials for accelerated MD, density-functional tight binding (DFTB), density functional theory.
